# Demonstration Turbine Maintenance Guide

This is fictional sample material created only for the RAG prototype. It is not a Mitsubishi Power manual and must not be used to operate real equipment.

## Bearing vibration

An increase in bearing vibration should be investigated as a trend rather than from one isolated reading. The engineer should first verify the sensor signal, compare the value with the machine's normal operating baseline, and check whether the change coincides with a load or speed transition. Relevant checks include lubrication pressure and temperature, bearing metal temperature, shaft alignment indicators, and recent maintenance activity.

If vibration rises rapidly or exceeds the site's approved operating limit, follow the site's escalation and shutdown procedure. Potential contributors include imbalance, misalignment, looseness, oil degradation, or a faulty sensor. A diagnosis must combine vibration spectrum information with operating conditions and inspection evidence.

## Exhaust-temperature spread

Exhaust-temperature spread is the difference between thermocouple readings around the exhaust. A widening spread may indicate uneven combustion, a thermocouple problem, fuel-nozzle contamination, or airflow variation. Confirm instrumentation health before attributing the change to combustion hardware. Compare the pattern with load, ambient conditions, fuel properties, and recent maintenance.

## Compressor fouling

Compressor fouling can gradually reduce mass flow and efficiency. Possible indicators include lower compressor discharge pressure, reduced output at comparable ambient conditions, and changes in heat rate. Engineers should compare corrected performance against a clean-machine baseline. Online or offline washing should only be scheduled according to the approved maintenance procedure and an engineering assessment.

## Investigation record

Record the timestamp, unit operating mode, load, ambient conditions, alarms, relevant sensor trends, maintenance history, and actions taken. Preserve the evidence used in the conclusion. Distinguish observed facts from hypotheses and document any uncertainty or missing data.
